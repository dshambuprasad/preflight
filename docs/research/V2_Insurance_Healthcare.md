# V2 — Insurance and Healthcare/Diagnostics as Pre-Send Communications Compliance Verticals

Research pass to assess two Indian verticals — insurance (IRDAI) and healthcare/diagnostics — as candidates for a pre-send communications compliance check, for comparison against lending (researched separately in R1). Primary sources (irdai.gov.in, mondaq summaries of the actual regulation text, nmc.org.in) preferred over blogs; where only secondary commentary was reachable, that is flagged.

Labels used throughout: **[fact]** = directly sourced and traceable to a primary or credible secondary document; **[inference]** = reasonable reading built on facts but not itself stated in a source; **[hypothesis]** = untested guess flagged as such. **COULD NOT ESTABLISH** used where search did not surface a reliable answer.

## VERTICAL A — INSURANCE (IRDAI)

### A1. Current instrument in force (2026)

**[fact]** The IRDAI (Insurance Advertisements and Disclosure) Regulations, 2021 ("Erstwhile Ad Regulations") — notified 7 April 2021, in force 9 April 2021 — has been **repealed and replaced**. The current framework (confirmed via Tuli & Co / Mondaq, 17 Sept 2024, citing the regulation numbers directly) is spread across three instruments effective April 2024–onward:
- IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests, Operations and Allied Matters of Insurers) Regulations, 2024 ("PPHI Regulations"), effective 1 April 2024
- IRDAI "Master Circular on Operations and Allied Matters of Insurers," 19 June 2024
- IRDAI "Master Circular on Protection of Interest of Policyholders," 5 September 2024
- Plus product-specific ad norms under the IRDAI (Insurance Products) Regulations, 2024

Sources: [Mondaq/Tuli & Co, "Looking Ahead: The Future of Insurance Advertising Norms in India"](https://www.mondaq.com/india/advertising-marketing-branding/1518396/looking-ahead-the-future-of-insurance-advertising-norms-in-india), [TaxGuru summary of 2021 Regs](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/irdai-insurance-advertisements-disclosure-regulations-2021.html), [irdai.gov.in document detail](https://irdai.gov.in/document-detail?documentId=397359).

**[inference]** This is a live, currently-in-force regime, not a lapsed one — it was reformed as recently as 2024, meaning the obligation set is fresh and the regulator is still bedding it in operationally.

### A3. Mandatory internal advertisement committee / prior-approval process

**[fact]** Yes — this is explicit and detailed, not implied. Per the PPHI Regulations/Operations Master Circular (via Mondaq/Tuli & Co, which quotes the regulation clauses):

- Every insurer must set up a **Board-approved Advertisement Committee**, replacing what used to be the "compliance officer" sign-off role for advertisements specifically (the compliance officer role continues for distribution-channel-issued ads).
- Composition: minimum 2 Key Management Persons (KMPs) + 3 senior officials one level below KMP + a permanent invitee from the **Product Management Committee (PMC)**.
- Quorum: 3 members, must include 1 KMP + the PMC invitee.
- The Committee's approvals must "align with the specified framework and the approvals given by the PMC on such products" — i.e., ad approval is explicitly tied back to product design sign-off.
- The Committee must "establish and maintain a system of control over the content, form and method of dissemination of advertisements" and maintain advertisement records under the BAAP.
- **All advertisements issued by insurers and distribution channels must be internally approved** (replacing the old regulator-filing requirement).
- Exemptions for agents from needing prior written approval: ads developed by the insurer and handed to agents as-is; purely generic ads (agent's own name/logo/address/phone only); simple correct statements about lines of insurance available.

Source: [Mondaq/Tuli & Co, 17 Sept 2024](https://www.mondaq.com/india/advertising-marketing-branding/1518396/looking-ahead-the-future-of-insurance-advertising-norms-in-india); corroborated by earlier description of the 2021-regime Advertisement Committee in [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/irdai-pphi-operations-allied-matters-insurers-regulations-2024-advertising.html).

**[inference]** This is the single most important fact for the deliverable's purpose: insurance already has a **mandatory, named, Board-level pre-approval body with a defined quorum**. A pre-send compliance check product would be entering a market that already has an incumbent internal process (validation that the workflow step exists) — but also a defined interface to slot into (the Advertisement Committee's own controls over "content, form and method of dissemination" is literally a system a machine check could sit inside or alongside).

### A4. Agent and intermediary communications — what can an agent say, and insurer liability

**[fact]** Section 42(3)(h)–(6) area of the Insurance Act, 1938 (as amended by the Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015) establishes:
- Agents must follow a Code of Conduct specified by IRDAI regulations. Violation disqualifies the agent under s.42(3)(h).
- **"The insurer shall be responsible for all the acts and omissions of its agents including violation of code of conduct... and liable to a penalty which may extend to one crore rupees."**
- Any insurer that appoints/uses an unlicensed person as an agent is itself liable to a penalty up to ₹1 crore.

Sources: [Indiankanoon, Section 42, Insurance Act 1938](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1439441/); corroborated in secondary commentary at [irdai.gov.in document detail](https://irdai.gov.in/document-detail?documentId=373502).

**[fact]** Agent Code of Conduct content (from IRDAI regulations under s.42(3)(h)): agents are prohibited from misrepresenting policy benefits/returns to a prospect; must not encourage prospects to misrepresent or hide material information on the proposal form; must not submit false documents (treated as fraud, can trigger both policy cancellation and agent license cancellation). Breach can lead to suspension or cancellation of the agent's license. Source: [Business Standard summary of Code of Conduct](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-cm/code-of-conduct-for-insurance-agents-irdai-115072401401_1.html), [insurancemocktest.in explainer of IRDAI Reg. 8](https://insurancemocktest.in/Code-of-Conducts/Code-of-Conduct-for-Insurance-Agents.php).

**Answer to "is the insurer liable for a mis-selling message sent by an agent": [fact] Yes, explicitly and by statute** — not by inference or case law, but by direct text of s.42 making the insurer "responsible for all acts and omissions of its agents." This is a genuine **principal-liable-for-agent** structure written into the primary Act, not just regulation. **[inference]** This is a stronger and cleaner liability chain than most retail-lending DSA/agent arrangements typically rely on (which are more often contractual indemnities rather than statutory principal liability) — worth flagging as a comparative strength for the insurance vertical.

### A6. The 26,667 mis-selling complaints, FY25 — verification and channel breakdown

**[fact] Verified directly.** Grievances categorised under "unfair business practices" (IRDAI's mis-selling bucket) against **life insurers** rose to **26,667 in FY25**, up **14.3% year-on-year** from **23,335 in FY24**. This represented **22.14%** of total complaints against life insurers (up from 19.33% the year before). Overall grievances against life insurers actually *declined* slightly to 120,429 (from 120,726) — meaning mis-selling is growing as a *share* of a roughly flat total complaint pool. Source: IRDAI Annual Report FY25, as reported in [Business Standard, 1 Jan 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/insurance/misselling-grievances-against-life-insurers-up-14-in-fy25-says-irdai-126010101087_1.html) and corroborated by [Insurance Business Asia](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/breaking-news/irdai-sees-misselling-complaints-rise-amid-stable-grievances-561149.aspx) and [KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/sectors/irdai-flags-mis-selling-complaints-jump-14-in-fy25-asks-insurers-to-probe-root-causes).

**[fact]** Figure is specific to **life insurers** — general/health insurance mis-selling is tracked separately and was not the number quoted (the FY25 Bima Bharosa portal recorded ~257,000 total grievances across all lines, of which ~120,000 were life and ~137,000 general/health, per secondary aggregation — **[inference]** the 26,667 is a subset of the ~120,000 life-insurance bucket, not of the full 257,000).

### A7. Who owns compliance; is there a documented pre-approval workflow

**[fact]** Under the IRDAI Corporate Governance for Insurers Regulations, 2024, every insurer must appoint a **Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)** as a Key Management Person, one level below the MD/CEO, with:
- minimum fixed tenure of 3 years; no vacancy for a continuous period >180 days
- explicit independence requirement — "should not have any reporting relationship with business verticals"
- responsibility for monitoring regulatory compliance generally, AML/CFT adherence, compliance risk assessment, and being the designated officer for filings/returns/approvals to IRDAI
- annual compliance status report to IRDAI plus a separate CCO certificate
- appointed by the Board on Nomination & Remuneration Committee recommendation

Source: [Lexology summary of IRDAI Corporate Governance for Insurers Regulations 2024](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=118dae25-8fbb-4318-b0db-a65954f49be4), [JSA newsletter](https://www.jsalaw.com/newsletters-and-updates/irdai-issues-regulations-on-corporate-governance-for-insurers/).

**[fact]** Per Section A3 above, the CCO's *advertisement-specific* sign-off authority was explicitly **transferred to the new Advertisement Committee** for insurer-issued ads (2024 reform), while the compliance officer's role **continues** for ads issued by distribution channels. So there are, in effect, **two named, documented pre-approval pathways**: the Advertisement Committee (insurer-originated ads) and the compliance officer (distribution-channel-originated ads).

**[inference]** This is a mature, well-specified compliance function with budget and headcount implied by regulation (KMP-level role, independence mandate, minimum tenure) — a pre-send tool selling into this vertical would be selling *into* an existing governance structure and specific named owners (CCO, Advertisement Committee secretary/members), not evangelizing the need for a compliance function from scratch.

## VERTICAL B — HEALTHCARE / DIAGNOSTICS

### B1. Regulatory patchwork governing hospital/lab marketing communications

There is **no single instrument** analogous to IRDAI's PPHI Regulations. The obligation set is fragmented across at least four legal sources, one of which is **currently not in force**.

A pre-send compliance product for hospitals would be building against a **moving, not-yet-final target**, which is a materially different (weaker, more prospective) foundation than insurance's.

### B2. Patient data — DPDP treatment of health data as special/sensitive category

**[fact]** The DPDP Act, 2023 **does not create a distinct "sensitive personal data" tier** the way GDPR does (confirmed by multiple independent legal summaries — [CookieYes](https://www.cookieyes.com/blog/india-digital-personal-data-protection-act-dpdpa/), [EY India](https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/cybersecurity/decoding-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023)). Health data is **not** given a separately-labelled special category in the Act's text. **[fact]** However, the DPDP Rules, 2025 (notified 14 Nov 2025) layer additional practical safeguards onto health data indirectly: heightened consent/security expectations, and — specific to healthcare entities — **Schedule 4 exemptions**: clinical establishments, mental-health establishments, registered medical practitioners, and allied health professionals are exempted from the *verifiable-consent* obligation, but only for processing "necessary and broadly restricted to providing health services and support for healthcare treatment" — a narrow, purpose-bound carve-out, not a blanket exemption. Sources: [Wikipedia, DPDP Rules 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Rules,_2025), [dpdpa.com, Schedule 4 text](https://www.dpdpa.com/schedule/schedule4.html), [Foxmandal, "Children's Data: Rethinking Schedule IV Exemptions"](https://foxmandal.in/childrens-data-rethinking-schedule-iv-exemptions/).

**[fact]** Hospitals that qualify as **Significant Data Fiduciaries** (by volume/sensitivity of data processed — health data is explicitly cited by multiple compliance-advisory sources as a likely trigger) must appoint a Data Protection Officer and run continuous audits. **[fact]** Rule 4 (Consent Management, including the Consent Manager registration framework relevant to health-data-sharing architectures like ABDM) takes effect **one year after notification — i.e., November 2026** — meaning key operative machinery is not yet live even now.

**[fact]** Sector-specific: the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) Health Data Management Policy (Ministry of Health and Family Welfare / National Health Authority) is a **consent-based architecture** — health information exchange between providers requires patient consent via a dedicated "Consent Manager" building block; ABDM keeps data with the "original custodian" (federated, not centralized) rather than pooling it. Source: [india.gov.in ABDM Health Data Management Policy listing](https://www.india.gov.in/category/health-wellness/subcategory/health-care-promotion-products/details/health-data-management-policy-of-ayushman-bharat-digital-mission-abdm), [Tandfonline assessment of ABDM](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23288604.2024.2392290). **[inference]** ABDM's consent architecture governs *health record exchange between providers*, not outbound patient marketing communications specifically — it is adjacent to, but not the same regulatory surface as, a pre-send marketing-message compliance check.

**[fact]** The **statutory penalty ceiling** under the DMR Act, 1954 itself is trivial by 2026 standards: first offence up to 6 months' imprisonment or fine (no rupee floor/ceiling specified in the original Act's text as retrieved); second offence up to 1 year. **[fact]** A 2020 draft Bill to raise this to ₹10 lakh (first offence, up to 2 years) / ₹50 lakh (repeat, up to 5 years) and expand the prohibited-disease schedule from 54 to 78 conditions and explicitly cover internet/electronic media **remains a draft — not introduced in Parliament as of the sources reviewed**. Source: [Mondaq draft bill summary](https://www.mondaq.com/india/food-and-drugs-law/937692/draft-drugs-and-magic-remedies-objectionable-advertisements-amendment-bill-2020), [MoHFW draft PDF](https://mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Draft%20of%20the%20Drugs%20and%20Magic%20Remedies.pdf). **[inference]** This means the one law that most directly targets misleading *disease-cure* claims in healthcare advertising carries a penalty ceiling most large hospital chains or diagnostic labs would treat as immaterial — a meaningfully weaker deterrent than IRDAI's crore-scale orders.

**COULD NOT ESTABLISH**: a named, penalized enforcement action against a large corporate hospital chain or diagnostic lab (e.g., Apollo, Fortis, Max, Manipal, Dr Lal PathLabs, Metropolis, Thyrocare) specifically for a health-check-package marketing communication. ASCI's healthcare rulings, where identifiable, skew toward smaller clinics, wellness centres, and AYUSH/alternative-medicine advertisers rather than the large listed hospital/diagnostics chains. **[inference]** This may indicate large chains are more compliance-careful, or that ASCI complaint/ruling data on large-chain-specific cases simply wasn't surfaced by this search pass — treat as a gap, not a conclusion either way.

### B4. Do Indian hospital chains/labs run high-volume marketing? Evidence of volume and channels

**[fact]** Yes, substantiated with real numbers:
- Dr Lal PathLabs: **ad spend rose from ₹31 crore (2021) to ₹37 crore (2022)**; the company processes **~94 million samples/year** (FY24 figure cited elsewhere as 78.2 million / 7.82 crore samples) and serves **~27.6–30 million patients/year** through 300+ labs, 7,500+ patient service centres, 13,500+ pickup points — a subset of these patient touchpoints generates outbound communication (results notifications, package upsell, repeat-test reminders). Source: [Statista via search summary](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1466514/dr-lal-pathlabs-number-of-tests-conducted/), [Forbes India profile](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/leadership/how-dr-lal-pathlabs-stands-the-test-of-time/93962/1).
- Industry-wide digital diagnostics competition (Dr Lal PathLabs, Thyrocare, Redcliffe Labs, Metropolis, 1mg, PharmEasy) is explicitly described in trade commentary as running "aggressive digital marketing" that has "compressed pricing" and "raised the cost of patient acquisition" — i.e., high, competitive campaign volume is an industry-recognized dynamic, not a one-off. **[inference, not directly sourced to a number]** No hard per-company message-volume figure (emails/SMS/push sent per month) was retrievable.
- Health-tech apps at meaningful download/user scale: 1mg 10M+ downloads, Practo 5M+ downloads — these are platforms that by nature run continuous notification/marketing campaigns (order reminders, health package promotions), and were the specific subject of the ASCI/Parallel dark-patterns study and the Nov 2025 CCPA self-audit exercise.
- Corporate hospital chains: typical digital-marketing spend cited in trade sources as **3–6% of OPD/IPD revenue**, rising for new-unit launches or medical-tourism pushes, with higher-margin service lines (cardiology, oncology) justifying 2–3x that spend — **[inference]** this is trade-press modeling, not an audited disclosure, and should be treated as directional rather than a hard fact.

**COULD NOT ESTABLISH** an exact aggregate message-volume figure (e.g., "X million SMS/emails sent per month by Indian hospital chains") comparable in specificity to the IRDAI FY25 complaint count.

### B5. Who owns compliance inside a hospital chain / diagnostic lab

**[fact]** Apollo Hospitals has a named Group Compliance function and a Company Secretary/Compliance Officer role at the individual-hospital-entity level (e.g., "AVP-Corporate Affairs and Legal (Company Secretary and Compliance Officer), Indraprastha Apollo Hospitals") and a separately-named **Group Chief Marketing Officer**. Source: [ZoomInfo/RocketReach listings surfaced via search](https://rocketreach.co/priya-ranjan-email_114367138).

**[inference]** Unlike insurance, where the CCO and Advertisement Committee roles are **regulator-mandated** (named in a specific regulation, with prescribed composition, tenure, and independence rules), the hospital-chain compliance/marketing-approval structure found here is **corporate-governance-driven, not sector-regulation-driven** — i.e., it exists because it's a listed company with standard company-secretary/compliance obligations under the Companies Act/SEBI LODR, not because a healthcare-specific regulator mandated a marketing-approval committee. **COULD NOT ESTABLISH** whether Apollo, Max, Fortis, or the major diagnostic labs have a *documented, marketing-specific* pre-approval workflow analogous to IRDAI's Advertisement Committee — no such document or policy reference was found in this search pass, and its absence in search results is itself weak evidence (not proof) that no such formal, sector-mandated structure exists.

## COMPARISON SCORECARD

| Criterion | Insurance (IRDAI) | Healthcare/Diagnostics |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Enforcement: present-tense or prospective?** | **Present-tense.** PPHI Regulations 2024 are in force now; IRDAI issued ~14+ named penalty orders in 2024 alone (Warnings and Penalties register), plus ₹5cr (Policybazaar, Aug 2025) and ₹1cr (Reliance General, Dec 2025) orders touching advertising/mis-selling-adjacent conduct directly. | **Mostly prospective / weak-present.** The one instrument purpose-built for corporate-hospital advertising (NMC RMP Regs 2023) is **suspended**, pending a Supreme Court case and an NMC panel report not yet finalized. What *is* enforced today (DMR Act, PCPNDT) carries token statutory penalties (DMR: 6 months/fine, unspecified amount, in force since 1954; a 2020 bill to raise this to ₹10–50 lakh remains undrafted-into-law) and is enforced mostly against small/AYUSH advertisers via ASCI (a voluntary, non-binding self-regulatory body), not against the large chains a pre-send tool would target. |
| **(b) Principal-liable-for-agent structure?** | **Yes, explicit and statutory.** Insurance Act s.42: insurer "responsible for all acts and omissions of its agents," penalty up to ₹1 crore. Clean, direct, unambiguous. | **No equivalent found.** Hospitals/labs don't have a comparable "principal liable for referring doctor's/marketing-partner's statements" statute surfaced in this research. Liability runs mostly to the individual professional (NMC/state medical council action against the doctor) or the specific establishment under DMR/PCPNDT for its *own* ads, not a clean vicarious-liability chain for third-party intermediary communications. **COULD NOT ESTABLISH** a healthcare equivalent of Insurance Act s.42. |
| **(c) Compliance function with budget already exists?** | **Yes, regulator-mandated.** Chief Compliance Officer is a named KMP role under IRDAI Corporate Governance Regulations 2024 (fixed 3-year minimum tenure, independence mandate, board-level reporting), plus a separate Board-approved Advertisement Committee with prescribed composition/quorum specifically for ad sign-off. | **Exists, but generic-corporate not sector-mandated.** Company Secretary/Compliance Officer roles exist at listed hospital chains (Companies Act/SEBI-driven), and a CMO function clearly exists — but no healthcare-regulator-mandated, ad-specific committee analogous to the Advertisement Committee was found. |
| **(d) Message volume** | **High and growing**, but complicated: a meaningful share of the mis-selling problem (bancassurance, agent pitches) is anecdotally **verbal**, not a written/digital artifact a pre-send tool would intercept. Written/digital insurance ad volume itself (product ads, renewal comms, cross-sell) is large but no single aggregate figure was found. | **High and demonstrably growing** in the specific channels a pre-send tool covers best: SMS/email/push/app notifications for health-check packages, diagnostic reminders, and health-tech app engagement (1mg 10M+, Practo 5M+ downloads; Dr Lal PathLabs ₹37cr ad spend, ~94M samples/patient touchpoints/year). This is comparatively *more* natively digital-message-shaped than insurance's verbal-heavy mis-selling problem. |
| **(e) Machine-checkable vs. judgment share of obligations** | **Mixed, moderate-to-high checkable share.** Registered-name presence, rating/ranking sourcing disclosure, superlative/guarantee-word detection, and risk-disclosure-presence are automatable; "claims beyond the policy's ability to deliver" and "matches policy provisions" require cross-referencing actual policy wording — real but bounded judgment work. | **Mixed, with a narrow but very clean automatable subset.** DMR Act's disease-claim-schedule matching and PCPNDT's sex-determination keyword domain are close to ideal machine-checkable problems (defined term lists, binary presence/absence) — arguably *cleaner* than insurance's context-dependent checks. But the harder, broader "misleading health claim"/"exaggerated cure" judgment calls (the majority of ASCI's actual complaint volume) are not reducible to keyword matching. |
| **(f) Mandatory pre-approval workflow already in place (validation + incumbent)?** | **Yes — strong validation, strong incumbent.** The Advertisement Committee (insurer ads) and compliance officer (distribution-channel ads) are named, regulator-mandated, and already operating. A pre-send tool would need to integrate with or supplement an existing, well-specified process — good validation of need, but also a real incumbent to displace or plug into. | **No consistent equivalent found.** No sector-mandated pre-approval committee for hospital/lab marketing was identified; approval structures where they exist appear to be internal/ad hoc (marketing + legal/compliance sign-off, unspecified in public sources) rather than a documented, regulator-shaped workflow. This is a *weaker* incumbent to displace, but also weaker validation that the market has already concluded such a workflow is necessary. |

### Bottom line read

**[inference]** Insurance presents the stronger case on regulatory clarity, statutory agent-liability, and an already-mandated (if incumbent) approval workflow — but a real share of its highest-profile compliance problem (mis-selling) may occur in verbal/point-of-sale conversations outside a pre-send text/creative tool's reach, and the sub-checks that remain (ad content, ranking claims) sit inside a regime that just finished a 2024 overhaul, meaning insurers' internal processes may still be in flux and receptive to new tooling — or alternatively resistant to yet another new requirement to absorb.

**[inference]** Healthcare/diagnostics presents higher, more clearly digital-native message volume and a narrower, cleaner automatable core (DMR Act disease-claim lists, PCPNDT keyword domain) — but its most relevant hospital-specific advertising rule is currently suspended and being fought out in the Supreme Court, actual enforcement teeth (DMR Act penalties) are close to nominal and predate the digital-advertising era, and there is no statutory analogue to insurance's agent-liability clause or its mandated Advertisement Committee. Enforcement that does exist (ASCI) is non-binding self-regulation and appears concentrated on smaller/AYUSH advertisers rather than the large hospital/diagnostics chains that would be the likely commercial target.
